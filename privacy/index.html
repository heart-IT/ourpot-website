OurPot — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2 October 2026
Applies to: the OurPot mobile app for iOS and Android (com.ourpot.app).
Provided by: heartIT ("we", "us").
Contact: rahulgarg_su@proton.me

OurPot is a private household expense notebook. It runs entirely on your phones.

  • No accounts. No server. No cloud. You never sign up, and there is no OurPot server that your data passes through or lives on.
  • We don't collect anything. No analytics, no telemetry, no advertising, no tracking, no third-party SDKs that phone home. We have no database of users because we have no users in the account sense.
  • Your ledger lives on your devices. Expenses, categories, receipts, and your household identity are stored locally and synced directly between the phones in your household.
  • Sync is peer-to-peer and encrypted in transit. Devices find each other over a public peer-to-peer network and connect directly; the data they exchange is encrypted so only your household's devices can read it.

The rest of this document explains the details, including the one place reality is more nuanced than "nothing leaves your phone": connecting two phones over the internet necessarily exposes their network addresses to the infrastructure that helps them find each other.

1. Who the "data controller" is

OurPot has no central operator that holds your data, so there is no controller in the usual sense for the contents of your ledger — you and the members of your household are. heartIT publishes the app and is responsible for this policy and for the app's code, but does not receive, store, or have access to your ledger, identity keys, or receipts.

2. What information OurPot handles, and where it lives

Everything below is created by you and stored on your own device(s). None of it is sent to us.

Category Examples Where it's stored Leaves your device?
Ledger entries amounts, currency, category, note, who paid, pooled/split mode, dates Locally, in the app's private storage Only to other devices in your own household, encrypted in transit
Receipts photos you choose to attach to an entry Locally Same as ledger entries — household devices only, never uploaded to us or any third party
Household identity your cryptographic keys and 24-word recovery phrase The device's secure storage (iOS Keychain / Android Keystore-backed secure store) The recovery phrase never leaves the device automatically. Public keys are shared with your household devices as part of normal sync
Membership which devices are members of your household Locally + synced to household devices Household devices only
App preferences local UI/app settings Locally No

OurPot does not ask for or collect your name, email, phone number, contacts, location, advertising identifiers, or any account credentials — because it has no accounts.

3. What we collect about you: nothing

We want to be precise because most apps cannot say this:

  • No analytics or usage statistics. The app contains no analytics SDK and sends no usage events anywhere.
  • No crash reporting service. Crash and diagnostic information stays on your device unless you explicitly choose to share it (see §6).
  • No advertising and no ad networks.
  • No third-party trackers or marketing SDKs.
  • No sale or sharing of personal information — there is nothing to sell or share, and we never will.

4. How sync works, and what is exposed on the network

This is the one area where "nothing leaves your phone" needs an honest footnote.

To keep your household ledger in sync without a server, your devices connect to each other directly over a public peer-to-peer network (built on the Hyperswarm / Hypercore protocols). Three things follow from that:

  • Discovery and connection use public peer-to-peer infrastructure. To find your other household devices and punch through home routers/firewalls, your device contacts public distributed-hash-table (DHT) nodes and may route the initial handshake through public relay nodes. These are decentralized, community/operator-run nodes — not an OurPot server — but they are third parties on the network path.
  • Your IP address is visible to peers and to that infrastructure. As with any networked app (including ordinary client–server apps), establishing a connection reveals your device's network address to the peers it connects to and to the discovery/relay infrastructure. An observer who already knows your household's public sync identifier could learn that a device is online and its approximate network location. The contents of your ledger are encrypted in transit and are not readable by this infrastructure or by uninvited peers — only by devices you have added to your household.
  • App updates travel over a separate peer-to-peer channel that we do seed. OurPot can update its app code without a store release, and it does that peer-to-peer too. A released build joins one worldwide discovery topic — the update drive published by us, the same topic for every OurPot install — and fetches the app bundle from whichever peers hold it, including the always-online machines we run so the drive stays available. Connecting to one of those machines reveals your device's IP address to us, in the same way that any app's update check reveals it to that app's publisher. Two limits on what that can tell us: no ledger data travels on this channel — it carries the app bundle, in one direction, and nothing of yours goes out — and the app joins it under a throwaway key generated per launch, unrelated to your household identity, so the connection is not linkable to you or to your household. We keep no logs of it.

Household phones can also sync directly over Bluetooth when they are near each other — only if you turn on Sync nearby over Bluetooth in Settings, which is off by default. That path uses no network at all: no DHT, no relay, no IP address, and it works with the internet switched off. The connection is encrypted the same way as every other sync, and nothing is collected. See §7 for the permission.

We do not operate the DHT or relay infrastructure your household sync depends on, do not log it, and receive nothing from it. The update drive is the one piece of network infrastructure we do run, and it is disclosed above rather than folded into that denial. If you are in an environment where exposing your IP address is a concern, you can use OurPot in solo mode (a single device, no sync) — note that solo mode stops household sync but not the update channel, so only an offline device or a VPN covers that.

5. Sharing within your household

When you invite another device into your household, you are choosing to share your ledger with the person who controls that device. From that point:

  • New entries, edits, receipts, and membership changes sync to all household devices.
  • Pairing uses a one-time invite plus an out-of-band verification fingerprint you read aloud to confirm you're connecting to the right device. Read it aloud — it's the protection against connecting to the wrong party.
  • Removing/revoking a device stops it from making future changes, but it does not reach into and erase data already stored on a device someone else physically controls (see §8).

OurPot is built for cooperative households (couples, families, roommates). It is not designed to protect members of the same household from each other.

6. Diagnostics you choose to share

For troubleshooting, Settings offers "Share diagnostic report" and "Recent crashes". These are never sent automatically and never sent to us. When you tap them, the app hands a file to your phone's normal share sheet, and you decide where it goes (e.g. emailing a maintainer).

The diagnostic bundle is redacted by design: it includes coarse technical metadata (command types, short identifiers, timestamps, payload sizes), a few connectivity readings taken at the moment you export it (whether syncing is running, whether your network lets devices connect directly, how many devices are connected right now, and whether Bluetooth is on and in what state), recent on-device crash logs, and a tail of the app's own technical log (the warn/error lines it writes for itself, redacted as they are written) — but not your individual entries, notes, payer identities, or receipts (a consistency-check diagnostic can carry a monthly total, and the in-app consent sheet says so). The connected-device figure is a count only — the devices' own identities are deliberately left out. You control whether to share it and with whom.

7. Device permissions and why OurPot asks

OurPot requests the minimum it needs, and only at the moment a feature needs it:

  • Camera — to scan an invite QR code from another household device, and to photograph a receipt when you attach one. OurPot does not record audio or video.
  • Photo library — only when you attach an existing photo as a receipt. OurPot reads the specific photo you pick; it does not scan your library.
  • Local network (iOS) — to discover and connect to your other household devices on the same Wi-Fi for faster, server-free sync.
  • Bluetooth — only if you turn on Sync nearby over Bluetooth in Settings. It lets household phones in the same room sync directly, even without internet. The connection is encrypted the same way as every other sync; nothing is collected, and OurPot never uses Bluetooth to work out where you are (on Android the scan permission is declared as never-for-location). Off by default.
  • Face ID / biometrics — used locally to confirm it's you before revealing your recovery phrase. Biometric data never leaves the device and is handled entirely by the operating system.
  • Vibration — for haptic feedback.

On Android, OurPot explicitly blocks audio-recording and overlay permissions. Legacy external-storage permissions remain declared for Android 12 and earlier by third-party dependencies (they are inert on Android 13+), but OurPot itself does not read or write shared external storage. OurPot also does not allow OS-level cloud backup of its data (allowBackup=false) so your ledger isn't copied off-device by the platform.

8. Keeping, deleting, and recovering your data

  • You hold the data. Because there is no server, deleting data is something you do on your own device(s).
  • Editing/voiding an entry. OurPot is a notebook: corrections are recorded as new entries that supersede or void the old one, so your household keeps an honest, auditable history. The original is marked voided/superseded rather than silently erased, and that history is visible to your household members.
  • Deleting everything locally. Settings → Reset this device wipes the ledger, identity keys, and receipts from that device. This is irreversible. Your 24-word phrase brings your identity back afterwards, but not the entries — see Recovery phrase below for where those can and cannot come from.
  • Other people's copies. Deleting your copy does not delete data already synced to another household member's device — they hold their own copy. To stop sharing, remove the member and/or stop syncing.
  • Recovery phrase. Your 24-word phrase restores who you are on a new device — your identity keys — not what you wrote down. Because there is no server, the entries themselves come back from one of two places: a household device that still holds them, or an encrypted backup file you exported beforehand (Settings → Export encrypted backup, offered while your household is still a single device — once you pair, the other device is the second copy). If every device is lost and no backup file survives, the entries are gone: we hold no copy to restore, and the phrase cannot recreate one. We cannot recover the phrase for you either — we never have it. Keep it safe.

9. Security

  • Connections between household devices are encrypted in transit.
  • Only devices you have admitted to your household can read your ledger; uninvited peers and the discovery/relay infrastructure cannot.
  • Your recovery phrase and identity keys are kept in the device's OS-backed secure storage (Keychain / Android Keystore).
  • We strongly recommend enabling a device passcode and biometric lock — on-device data is ultimately protected by your device's own security.

No system is perfectly secure, but OurPot's design deliberately minimizes what could be exposed: there is no central honeypot to breach, because there is no central store.

10. Children's privacy

OurPot is intended for general audiences managing a household budget and is not directed to children. We do not knowingly collect information from children — in fact, we do not knowingly collect information from anyone (§3).

11. International use and transfers

OurPot has no servers, so we perform no cross-border transfers of your data to or through us. Peer-to-peer sync sends your encrypted data directly between your own devices, which may be in different countries; the routing/discovery infrastructure (§4) is globally distributed. The data exchanged remains encrypted in transit and readable only by your household devices.

12. Your rights

Depending on where you live (e.g. GDPR/UK GDPR, CCPA/CPRA), you may have rights to access, correct, delete, or port your personal data. Because all of your data is in your own hands on your own devices, you can exercise these directly in the app: view it on the ledger, correct it with an edit, and erase it with Reset this device. We hold no copy to produce, rectify, or delete on your behalf. If you believe we nonetheless hold data about you, contact us at rahulgarg_su@proton.me.

13. Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we'll update the "Last updated" date and post the new version at https://ourpot.heartit.tech/privacy/. Material changes will also be noted in the app's release notes. Because OurPot collects nothing, changes will typically clarify wording rather than expand data use.
